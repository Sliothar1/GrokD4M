# Loughrea Semi-Final A Travesty On Sport

Connacht Sentinel, Tuesday 20 October 1953, page 3.
Courtesy of Irish Newspaper Archives: https://irishnewsarchive.com/?a=d&d=CSL19531020.1.3

Transcribed from the page image. No team lists are printed.

---

Maree 4-2 : Fohenagh 2-3

EAST is east and west is west and never the twain should meet, but they did at Loughrea on Sunday with near disastrous results for the lives and limbs of the players and the fair name of the national game.

From the opening salvos when exchanges disclosed that the match might prove a powder keg to the final skirmish which left half a dozen warriors with bloodied heads the affair was a travesty on sport.

The attendance was small and not without good cause. The recent record of such matches has been bad but the limit was reached on Sunday.

In short it was a really disgraceful exhibition. The public—and it still pays the piper—has become thoroughly disgusted with such apalling displays of bad temper and worse sportsmanship.

In between the "interludes for timber" the match had its moments and Fohenagh must be rated rather unlucky to lose.

They led by 1-3 to 0-0 at half-time but that advantage, with even ordinary luck, should have been double at that stage.

During the opening ten minutes of the second half the eastern team appeared to become rattled and, seizing their opportunity, Maree rammed home three telling goals to snatch a win when defeat for them seemed certain.

Short their "sheet anchor," Malachy Donnellan, Maree started very indifferently and but for the solid stopping of his brother, Mansy Donnellan, at full back, would quickly have been in a hopeless position.

Fast Fohenagh centrefielders Tony O'Gorman, a U.C.G. student, and Patsy Fitzgerald took command from the start.

Paddy O'Rourke and Brendan Hanniffy were the kingpins of the Maree attack. Wretched shooting on their part combined with some stylish back play by some Fohenagh defenders, who touched form far beyond intermediate standards, kept their score sheet clean to the interval.

Soon after the re-start Sean Grealy came on for F. Hanniffy and from that moment onwards nothing went right for the losers.

Maree had a fast goal but, nothing daunted, Fohenagh struck back to duplicate that effort and soon afterwards suffered the crowning misfortune of having a vital goal disallowed for a goal-mouth infringement.

From there on the losers went down hill fast and Maree gained a stranglehold on the match.

THE PLAY

J. Sweeney opened Fohenagh's account when he pointed a forty yards free and T. Glynn added a second. When F. Byrne pulled down a puck out from his own goals Sweeney snapped up the ball in his stride to flash in a Fohenagh goal. T. Sweeney closed the first-half scoring with Fohenagh's third point.

Almost from the throw-in Maree scored when the speedy P. O'Rourke beat two men and crashed home a great goal. Before the cheering had died, Fohenagh had negatived that effort with a splendid goal by T. Glynn and the game really came to life.

Maree forced a "70" which S. Finn finished for a point. From the puck out James Burke started another Maree attack and John Burns banged O'Rourke's centre to the net. Seconds later P. O'Rourke drove over a Maree point to leave his side but a single point behind. Excitement reached fever pitch as Sean Finn's goal put Maree in front for the first time.

Following an accidental injury to a player at centrefield a flare-up occurred and more firm action by referee E. Walsh at that stage may have averted subsequent "shennanigans."

In a fast break-away Fohenagh rushed through a goal but the umpires promptly signalled an infringement. Two final attacks by Fohenagh were firmly dealt with by Patch Holland and B. Hanniffy and against the run of play John Burns again goaled for Maree following a side-line throw-in.

Time was now running short and as the referee was about to call for the ball the battle royal started. Hurleys were used like stone age clubs and men from both sides fell as if poleaxed but providentially none was seriously injured.

After repeated failures to get both sides lined up again, the referee called it a day, leaving Maree winners by 4-2 to 2-3.
