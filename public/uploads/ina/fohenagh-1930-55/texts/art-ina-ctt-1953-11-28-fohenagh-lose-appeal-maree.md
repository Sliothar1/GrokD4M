# Fohenagh Lose Junior Hurling Tie Appeal

Connacht Tribune, Saturday 28 November 1953, page 26.
Courtesy of Irish Newspaper Archives: https://irishnewsarchive.com/?a=d&d=CTT19531128.1.26

Transcribed from the page image. The paper calls the game a "junior hurling semi final". The match reports of 3, 20 and 24 October 1953 call it the Intermediate County semi-final.

---

AN appeal by Fohenagh against the decision of the Galway Co. Board G.A.A. in awarding the Galway junior hurling semi final to Maree, was lost by three votes to two at a meeting of the Connacht Council G.A.A. in Castlerea. Senator Dan O Rourke, vice chairman, presided.

Fohenagh's claim was that the [game] was unfinished when the score was 4 2 to 2 3 in Maree's favour. Fohenagh were represented by Norman Farragher and Hugh Pender while Rev. Fr. M. Walsh, chairman, and Mr. J. Whelan, secretary, represented Galway Co. Board.

Senator O'Rourke said the referee in his report had stated that the game commenced at 3.15 p.m. and finished at 4.40 p.m. at Loughrea on October 18th. The referee had reported players who were swinging hurleys at each other and he should have put those players off the field, said Senator O'Rourke. The referee however could not say which player had started the fracas and as there was only one steward the referee had to clear the lines on several occasions. At the end of the game the referee had reported that after continuous blowing of the whistle only the Maree team appeared. The Civic Guards had informed the referee that they would not allow the game to continue and he told the Guards that he (referee) was only attempting to get the teams together to blow the final whistle. This final sentence in the referee's report took Fohenagh's case away altogether, said Senator O'Rourke. The time was up.

Fohenagh's delegates held that the game was unfinished. Frank Madden was captain and no one had approached him. The Fohenagh team was on the pitch the whole time attending to an injured player. The referee had not approached them.

Rev. Fr. Walshe said that at the Co. Board meeting there was a proposition that the match be declared finished and an amendment that the match be replayed. The voting was seven to four in favour of the match being declared finished.

"A HAMMERING MATCH?"

Senator O'Rourke: What would be the result if they met again? Would it be another hammering match?

Fohenagh delegate: There would not be anything like that. The players reported by the referee as swinging hurleys at each other did not strike each other.

Rev. Fr. Walsh: My attitude was I was going to be severe. Both myself and another delegate at the Co. Board meeting had suggested that both teams be suspended. We were the only two in favour of such a course.

Mr. J. Dunne (Galway) said the match was on for an hour and 25 minutes.

Chairman: That left a lot of time for stoppages. The only question is whether or not the match was finished.

Mr. E. Gilhooley (Sligo) said when the referee in his report stated he was about to blow the final whistle that knocked the bottom out of the appeal.

Mr. M. O'Callaghan (Roscommon) said there could have been a minute or two to play and that would have meant a big difference. The referee had not stated that Fohenagh were unwilling to line out. He proposed that the Council order a replay.

Mr. J. McTiernan (Sligo) seconded the proposal that the game be replayed.

"MATCH WAS FINISHED"

Chairman: It is my view that the match was finished. There must have been more than an hour's play assuming there were ten minutes for an interval there were fifteen minutes for stoppages and that is a long time for stoppages. It did not appear probable that that five points could be made up in a minute or two. There is of course a good deal of sympathy due to Fohenagh because there is room for a difference of opinion.

Fohenagh delegate: We did not get sixty minutes of hurling.

Mr. M. O'Callaghan (Roscommon): The Guards must have thought there was more playing time.

Rev. Fr. Walshe: If a replay was granted for this match spectators would remember it and if in future a team was being beaten in the closing stages that team's spectators could invade the pitch.

Mr. E. Gilhooley (Sligo): From the referee's report I thought it was Maree's spectators who invaded the pitch.

Rev. Fr. Walshe: The referee stated it was the players who started the fracas and then the spectators joined in.

Chairman: If there was a rule I would rule on the matter.

Mr. J. Dunne (Galway) proposed that Galway Co. Board's decision be upheld. Mr. E. Gilhooley (Sligo) seconded.

For Mr. Dunne's proposals—Messrs. Dunne, Gilhooley and Senator O'Rourke (3).

For Mr. O'Callaghan's proposal—Messrs. O'Callaghan and McTiernan.

The appeal was declared lost.
