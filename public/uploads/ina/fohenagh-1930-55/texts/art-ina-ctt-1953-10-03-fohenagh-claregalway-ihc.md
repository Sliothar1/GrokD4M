# Incidents Mar I.H. Quarter Finals

Connacht Tribune, Saturday 3 October 1953, page 22.
Courtesy of Irish Newspaper Archives: https://irishnewsarchive.com/?a=d&d=CTT19531003.1.22

Transcribed from the page image. Only the Fohenagh v Claregalway part is given here. The same article goes on to report Maree v Killimor.

---

I.H.: Fohenagh 4-7; Claregalway 4-6.
I.H.: Maree 3-6; Killimor 1-3.

THE current season cannot be rated a vintage one for county championship matches, particularly in the lower grades.

Sunday's poor programme at Loughrea, where Gardai had to intervene to quell riots during both matches did nothing to improve the standard.

Proceedings got off to a really bad start when the appointed referee failed to put in an appearance. The rival teams failed to agree on a suitable substitute until an alfresco debate had progressed for over half an hour.

Everything was then in order except for the slight detail of umpires and linesmen. Eventually six of the two hundred spectators present were persuaded by a member of the Co. Board to "volunteer" for the honour, and the Fohenagh v. Claregalway match was on.

Short their sheet-anchor and captain, Tom Flaherty, Claregalway put little heart into their business during the opening quarter and Fohenagh's "ace," Tim Sweeney, availed of their laxity to rattle up the tidy personal tally of two goals and four points.

A couple of good saves by W. Duggan in goals and solid stopping by veteran full back John Glennon and by centre half back Brian Fahy infused new confidence into the westerners and with closer marking they put a noticeable halt to Fohenagh's gallop while M. Duggan and J. Holland, a much improved player, scored four points.

FLARE-UP

A slight brush between two players in Claregalway's danger zone was the spark to start a flare-up involving several. The ever-ready "commandos" on the side line broke through the "sound barrier" to join in the fray which developed into modified mayhem before the Gardai and a few County Board officials succeeded in breaking it up.

With that much out of their systems the players lined up to re-start, but the ball was missing. Repeated appeals produced no ball and unofficial search parties combed the pitch but the elusive sphere could not be found. Eventually a new one was thrown in and the game was on once more.

Play was now more evenly contested and Fohenagh, still showing more confidence with a grand goal by M. Dooley, led by 3-6 to 0-4 at the break.

Claregalway came out from the re-start like new men. Gone was their loose marking and general listlessness — in its place driving determination. They laid down an incessant barrage on the Fohenagh goals but two splendid saves by goalie T. Clinton kept them at bay until a second row broke out.

This was promptly quashed by an alert Garda and Claregalway's ex-minor, Tom Wall, with a neat point put his side on the highroad to a sensational rally.

WALL'S FOUR GOALS

From the puck out the Fohenagh captain, Frank Madden, centred sweetly for his speedy colleague, A. O'Sullivan, to point, but that was to be Fohenagh's last score for twenty minutes. In the meantime Claregalway kept Wall plied with passes and, showing rare opportunism, he banged in four capital goals in rapid succession to put his side a point in front.

Fohenagh were now badly rattled but Tim Sweeney, doing the work of five in defence, at centre and in attack, rallied his men sufficiently to snatch the game out of the fire with a last-second goal by Dooley.

Claregalway: W. Duggan, T. Grealy, J. Glennon, R. Fahy, C. Grealish, B. Fahy, P. Feeney, T. Wall, Dan Duggan, M. Duggan, J. Holland, B. Raftery, M. Boyle, M. Monahan, D. Grealy.
