# Blackguardism at Athenry Causes Heat at County Board Meeting

Connacht Tribune, Saturday 13 June 1942, page 11 (printed page 5).
Courtesy of Irish Newspaper Archives: https://irishnewsarchive.com/?a=d&d=CTT19420613.1.11

EXTRACT, transcribed from the page image: the opening and the Fohenagh passages. The full report is in the clipping. The rest of the debate (motions, the final vote and other board business) is summarised at the end.

---

AFTER a heated discussion lasting over two hours, Galway County Board G.A.A. at their monthly meeting in the Royal Hotel, Galway on Saturday, decided to suspend for a year Claregalway and Fohenagh hurling teams which played in the semi-final of the 1941 Junior Championship at Athenry. As a consequence of the suspension of those two teams, Eyrecourt, who won the other semi-final, were formally declared 1941 champions by the chairman, Mr. T. O'Connor.

The matter arose when the Chairman read the report of the referee, Mr. W. Corbett. In his report Mr. Corbett stated that at first the game was fast with some keen, hard hurling on both sides. He saw players from both teams get into a fighting attitude. Spectators rushed into the pitch and some of the Fohenagh players were injured. He tried to clear the pitch, but could not and had to call the match off. He blamed the spectators for the trouble and recommended that the Board fix a replay.

Chairman: This is a serious report.

Mr. E. Walsh: The referee does not give the blame on either of the teams.

Mr. W. Callanan: The Fohenagh players were lucky to get out of Athenry with their lives that day.

Chairman: The Fohenagh players were called off the field by their officials.

Mr. W. Callanan: The referee told the Fohenagh players to leave Athenry for their own safety and also told them that he would recommend to the County Board that they be awarded the match. The situation must have got very dangerous when they could not clear the field.

After Mr. Callanan had asked and the Board had agreed to hear the Fohenagh captain, Michael Barrett,

Mr. Barrett said that he was coming out of the field when he met the referee, Mr. Corbett, who told him to get his men out of the field or they would be murdered.

During the match a man who was playing against him caught his hurley and struck him in the mouth. He was knocked to the ground and was kicked for about fifteen yards. The referee told him afterwards that he was awarding the match to Fohenagh.

"PANZER" DIVISIONS.

P. Ruane, captain of the Claregalway team, said the trouble started in the forward line. As he was playing full-back he could not see the actual incidents, but when the trouble started he pulled all his players out and lined them up in the centre of the pitch. He did not see a blow struck, nor a kick; all he saw was the crowd rushing on the pitch.

T. Gill, another of the Fohenagh players, said that they went to Athenry to play the game in the spirit in which it should have been played. He saw his captain being knocked on the ground and kicked. He never before had to run off a G.A.A. field, but he had to do it that day.

Chairman: It takes two to make a row. Only for certain people challenging other people to fight the row would have been smoothed over.

Mr. M. Silver (Ardrahan) said that some teams had powerful "panzer" divisions behind them and it was those blackcoats who caused all the trouble. He proposed that the Claregalway Club be suspended for two years as, in his opinion, a team was responsible for its followers.

...

Mr. T. Murphy: You only saw ten minutes of the game. One of the Fohenagh players is still maimed after that match.

...

[Summary of the rest of the report, not a transcription: members argued over whether to suspend one club or both. Mr. Silver's motion against Claregalway alone was defeated by eight votes to one. In the "Serious Charges" section the Chairman said that Fohenagh "were not playing a legal team that day", and Mr. W. Callanan objected to the remark. Mr. Walsh's proposal that both teams be suspended for twelve months was carried by six votes to three, and the Chairman declared Eyrecourt 1941 junior hurling champions.]
